Altitude

Some evenings I step out of myself for a minute. Not far. Just high enough to see the whole thing at once. From up there it looks almost simple. A man at a stove, stirring down tomatoes somebody else grew and let him pick. A man on a bike before the sun commits to anything. A man at a desk moving other people's language around. A man in a room full of celebration for someone he loves, holding his own glass, holding his own question. From that height you can't hear the noise. You can only see the pattern, and the pattern is a life, moving. Returning to the same few rooms. The same few people. The same page of a navy notebook where four lines wait like unlocked doors. The view is the easy part. The descent is the work.

Back in through the top, where the weather lives. It's loud up there, always forecasting. I've learned not to trust the first report. Then lower, into the chest, the gut, the places that don't speak in sentences. They tighten and loosen and that's the whole vocabulary. Lately I've been listening there first. The body knew about certain conversations before the calendar did. Lower still, to the hands. What they reach for. What they've learned to set down. And then the floor of it. The last stop, the one that doesn't flatter. Not what do I want. Something quieter and harder than that. The answer isn't always what I need, and what I need isn't always the answer, and standing in that gap without flinching is the practice now. Then I'm back. Feet on the kitchen floor, sauce still going, night coming on.

The view was good.

The ground is better.
