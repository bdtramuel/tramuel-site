People I know well have been dealing with health issues lately, mental and physical. People whose lives I’ve been part of, not just people whose updates I see. I notice their partners, spouses, and families showing up for them.

Most are in their thirties and forties. I’m 56.

I thought I needed to take better care of myself so I wouldn’t have to depend on anyone.

Then I had surgery.

Beforehand, I told my sister and my dad not to come. I explained that I was uneasy. I’d end up worrying about their comfort instead of my recovery, and I’m grumpy when I’m healing. I’d stock the apartment, get what I needed, and keep people on standby.

I wasn’t refusing help, exactly. I was arranging it so I wouldn’t need much.

In 2019, when Michelle, my then-wife, had foot surgery, I spent the days before it making comments about everything I’d have to do: missing work, the drive, the time of the surgery. I was joking, mostly.

Then I sat in the waiting room thinking about her and what life would mean without her. Afterward came the meals, the pharmacy runs, the trips up and down the stairs.

This time I was the patient.

The plan might have held if not for coffee with caramel.

A 10:30 arrival became leaving after 5:30. Lavante was there, went home during the delay, and came back before they took me in.

Somewhere in that long day, I stopped managing it. The delay took the choice away from me.

Since then, Lavante has dropped things off, texted every day, and offered to get me anything. She talked her way past the concierge even though she isn’t on my list. Five Two sat with me the next day and offered to change my bandages. My dad, my sisters, and my youngest son all showed care.

I felt loved.

I didn’t ask for any of it.

After my ear surgery in 2013, people on Twitter checked on me. Someone even left a freestyle on my blog. I wrote about the compassion of others.

That care was easy to receive. There was no one to host.

I keep coming back to that thought about taking better care of myself so I won’t have to depend on anyone. I still want to take better care of myself. I’m less sure about what I’m asking that care to guarantee.

Was telling my family not to come a boundary, or the old thing in better clothes?

I told them no, and there was cancer in my neck the whole time.
