My mom used to say I have Hugo Boss taste on a K-Mart budget.

She wasn't wrong.

In New Orleans, I walked into Orleans Shoe Co. looking for a suede brush for my Vibaes. They didn't have the brush for my style, so I ended up staring at a pair of men's Oncept sneakers. $300, of course. A classic runner with no obvious branding. I was worn out from walking back from Jackson Square, so I told them I loved them, and I'd be back. My suitcase was already stuffed, and I still had a gift to find for Five Two.

On my birthday, I came out of The Vintage, where I'd had champagne and beignets, and saw another shoe store across the street. I went right in. Same eye, same shoes. It took me a minute to realize it was the same store, a different location. I half-joked that it was a sign.

This one had a deeper selection, and I got more of the story behind Oncept. The Orleans Shoe Co. team goes to market twice a year, and this time they'd been set to meet with Golden Goose when Oncept pulled them in. They were sold, although I suspect Oncept was the one who got sold. They talked up the recycled materials and the guarantee, and showed me how to get a good fit.

I was still trying to talk myself into $300 when I noticed the Diadoras on the shelf below the first five rows… the Equipe Dirty SW Evo in Beige Dew, a 1975 runner with stonewashed suede and metal eyelets. $225 felt reasonable by comparison.

At the register, the woman helping me mentioned she was born in Roanoke. So was I. Raised there, too. She offered to mail the shoes home or recycle the box so it wouldn't take up room in my suitcase. I bought a pair of Feetures socks while I was at it, and sized down because I wear thin socks or none, so the fit is right.

She recycled the box.
