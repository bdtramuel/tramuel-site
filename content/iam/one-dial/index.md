I posted something on the tenth about flatness. Three sentences, and then I moved on, because the piece was supposed to be about joy and I didn't want to stand in that doorway too long. Here's the part I'll stay abstract about, because it's the tender one. I'd gotten used to a certain flatness. Not sad exactly. Just level. You can run a whole competent life on level, and I did, for a while, and nobody noticed but me.

I've been carrying that paragraph around since. Then I said it out loud in a room where saying things out loud is the entire assignment, and the person across from me took it, turned it over, and showed me the underside. I had been describing flatness like weather. Something that arrives.

She said it isn't weather. It's maintenance.

The word we landed on was squelching. Not dramatic suppression. Nothing that would look like anything from the outside. Small, constant, competent. Something happens, you feel the first edge of it, and you file it. You keep the meeting moving. You keep the household running. You keep your face doing what a face is supposed to do. Do that for enough years, and it stops being a decision. It becomes a setting.

Then comes the part that actually got me. It isn't selective. You don't get to turn down grief and leave joy at full volume.

It's one dial.

Push the hard things below the line, and everything else comes down with them, quietly, on the same slider. Joy doesn't leave. It gets thin. You still recognize it. Good news arrives, you say the right words, and something in you registers the event without registering the feeling.

That's the flatness. Not sadness. Not damage.

The running cost of managing myself well for a long time. What I noticed from the outside was the symptom. Level. Steady. Fine. Somebody else had to hand me the wiring diagram. I want to be careful here, because this is the place where a post like this usually turns into a comeback story. I'm not repaired. What changed is smaller than repair and more useful. I can feel myself doing it now.

There's a half-second when something lands, and I used to file it automatically, and now the filing has a sound to it. Sometimes I still file. But I hear it happen. On the days I don't, joy shows up at its full size, and it is almost too much. Nobody warns you that feeling things at full volume is loud.

I've had to relearn that the loudness is the point and not a malfunction. The other thing worth saying… the feelings I was squelching were never gone. They were late. They come back on a delay, at odd hours, attached to nothing in particular. A song in the car. A shift in the light on a Tuesday. Some of it belongs to things that happened years ago and are only now getting around to being felt.

I've stopped treating that as a problem to solve. It's just the mail catching up. In the last post I wrote that anticipation is a physical thing, that it sits behind the sternum, that it makes you get up earlier than you have to. All still true. What I understand now is why it left, and it wasn't because life got worse. It's because I got very good at not being moved. That skill has a bill. It came due quietly, in installments, over years, and I paid it without ever seeing the invoice.

Level was never neutral. It was the price.

One dial, I want to leave it turned up.
