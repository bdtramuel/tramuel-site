I’ve been reading more. Most of that time used to go to scrolling. Some of it went to the television.

Right now I’m in the middle of David Whyte’s *Consolations*, where each chapter is about one word. Next to it are Jamel Brinkley’s *A Lucky Man* and Lawrence Burney’s *No Sense in Wishing*, both from Better World Books instead of Amazon. A used copy of Jamaica Kincaid’s *A Small Place*. Frederick Joseph’s *Patriarchy Blues*, an ex-library copy. Ocean Vuong’s *On Earth We’re Briefly Gorgeous*. Beronda Montgomery’s *When Trees Testify*, borrowed from the actual library, and Robin Wall Kimmerer’s *Braiding Sweetgrass*. That’s two botanists in one stack. Baldwin and Kiese Laymon were already on the shelf.

Most of them are Black writers, which is on purpose, though it isn’t a rule.

Online it’s Aeon and Psyche, and Leo Babauta’s Zen Habits, which reads the way I try to write. The philosophy I read usually ends up shelved in self-help: Whyte, Ruiz, Hollis, Burkeman.

Reading to learn, reading to smile.
