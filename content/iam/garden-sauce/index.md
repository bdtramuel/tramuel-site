There wasn't a recipe.

Just a bag of rigatoni, a cutting board, and the quiet confidence that dinner would eventually introduce itself.

Whole tomatoes. Cherry tomatoes. Both picked from her garden. African basil still fragrant from the afternoon heat. Spicy vegan sausage with fennel. A wedge of real Parmesan waiting to be shaved instead of shaken from a plastic container.

The sauce didn't come from a jar. It came from paying attention.

While it simmered, the girls drifted in and out of the kitchen. Questions. Laughter. The kind of conversation that doesn't require everyone to be talking at once. She was outside training for a long ride, doing the work that nobody applauds while it's happening.

That left me inside.

Stirring. Tasting. Watching over the girls. Waiting for the tomatoes to surrender into something they weren't when they started.

Mincing garlic. Washing dishes before anyone asks. Keeping children company so someone else can chase a goal. Grating cheese from a block because it tastes better, even if it takes another minute.

Love doesn't always announce itself.

Sometimes it smells like basil.

Sometimes it sounds like a wooden spoon scraping the bottom of a pot.

Sometimes it's simply deciding to make something from what already exists instead of wishing for more.

I've noticed that the best meals I've ever made usually begin the same way.

No real plan.

Just enough care to start.

The ingredients figure out the rest.
