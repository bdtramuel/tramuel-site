Sunday morning. My own kitchen.

A candle burning. Not for light (it's daytime) but for the smell. For the ritual of striking the match.

Beans hitting the grinder. That first crackling roar. Then the kettle, then the bloom, the pour-over unfolding slowly. No music. No TV. Just the drip and the candle and me.

Breakfast took its time because it was allowed to.

A grits bowl. Creamy, peppered. Over-easy eggs laid on top, yolks ready to run. Veggie sausage seared in the pan. White cheddar melting into the grits where the heat tells it to. And biscuits. Homemade. Flour on the counter still, and I left it there.

I eat at my own table.

It has been a week of motion.

Both jobs. The one that pays the bills, and the one that pays the community. Part-time, small hours, but showing up. Two birthday dinners for people I love, candles and laughter and tables that weren't mine but I belonged at. The Charlotte Auto Show. The Harvey B. Gantt Center, walking slowly through the newest exhibits, letting good work have my attention.

I gave a lot of myself out this week.

Which is its own kind of good.

But today I brought it all back home. My place, my pan, my mug, my chair. After recovery, after giving, after moving through a full week, I'm inside my own walls doing exactly what I want at exactly the pace I choose.

Today I moved something else home.

My blog. Eighteen years of words. I carried them out of a rented corner of the internet and brought them back to a place I built myself. Keystroke by keystroke. It was meditative in a way I didn't expect: just me, the terminal, the quiet, and the steady clack of making something mine.

A lit candle.

Fresh-ground coffee, poured slowly.

A grits bowl with everything I love in it.

Biscuits from scratch.

Silence.

Keys striking.

And the feeling of arriving somewhere I built myself.

I Am.
