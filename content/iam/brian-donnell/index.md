I’m on Facebook again. The profile says Brian Donnell, with a throwaway email behind it. I signed up to buy things on Marketplace: a dining table, a leather chair, a bike, some art. A few people have found me. We’re friends now, as far as Facebook is concerned, and that’s the whole relationship. Twenty-five more are pending.

The word has been used so loosely for so long that people are careful with it now. We say fam, but they aren’t family. I have family, and I have friends, and both matter to me. We say team, but nobody’s playing a sport or trying to beat the other team at the call center.

I deleted my Facebook profile once. None of my friendships changed. People groaned, wanted to know why I left, and spammed the Instagram I kept. If someone leaving Facebook bothers you and they aren’t someone whose funeral you’d attend, it probably isn’t about Facebook.

It’s a small circle, the kind that fits around one table. We’ve taken trips together and buried family. We’ve gotten each other through jobs, marriages, and kids. We eat, we drink, and we tell the truth about money, wives, and the stuff that keeps us up at night. None of it happened in a comment section, and none of it needed one.

My friends are my friends. I talk to them when I’m not logged into anything.
