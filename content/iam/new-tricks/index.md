I'm not going to explain the whole thing. I've never been good at that, and I've stopped apologizing for it.

But something moved this year, and I want it on the record.

There's a number attached to my work that got bigger. Not because I turned into somebody else. Because I finally said the quiet part at normal volume, and somebody on the other side of the table did the math and agreed with me. At my age you're supposed to be coasting on what you already know. Turns out the old dog was just waiting for someone to hold up the treat and mean it.

I'm learning to pull people out of water. There'll be a card at the end of it with my name on it, which is funny, because there are versions of me from at least two different decades who'd call that either hilarious or impossible. Both of them were wrong. I like the idea of being the one who knows what to do at the edge of a pool.

There's a room I go to. Someone in it is very good at her job. She asks the question I've been walking laps around all week and then she waits, which is the part I didn't know I needed. Fit is everything. You can spend years in the wrong room being extremely articulate about nothing.

The body is in motion, and for once not as punishment. Bike. Pool. Sidewalk. Iron. And soon a gym where everybody yells in a dialect I don't speak yet, which I'm choosing to find charming rather than terrifying. The routine stopped being a chore somewhere around the time it started feeling like a friend who shows up early and waits in the car.

And in September, a city I've been meaning to meet since before I had the nerve. Mine. Alone. On the actual day. No permission slip, nobody's schedule but my own. I'd been saving that trip so long it had started to feel like a rumor. It's a boarding pass now.

Here's the part I'll stay abstract about, because it's the tender one. I'd gotten used to a certain flatness. Not sad exactly. Just level. You can run a whole competent life on level, and I did, for a while, and nobody noticed but me.

What I'd forgotten is that anticipation is a physical thing. It sits behind the sternum. It makes you get up earlier than you have to.

It's back. I'd like to be clear about how strange and how good that is.

Old dog, new tricks. Same yard, but I found a gap in the fence.
