Co-Star told me I can change my mind as often as I want. Which felt less like advice and more like a permission slip arriving late, after I'd already used it.

I've changed my mind about her fifty times. More, probably. I stopped counting when counting stopped being useful. Each time felt like evidence against me: proof that I hadn't moved, that I was circling the same block with better vocabulary.

But feelings revise themselves. A memory sharpens, then blurs, then sharpens again. Changing my mind might just mean I'm still paying attention. The mistake is treating every revision as a verdict.

A change of heart isn't a change of direction. That's the distinction I keep skipping. I can wake up tomorrow missing her and still not text her. I can remember, warmly, why it mattered and remember, clearly, why it ended. Both can be true by lunch. Neither requires a move.

I wrote recently that [ambiguity is good at passing for patience](https://www.tramuel.com/iam/best-served). Changing my mind is its cousin. It can pass for openness. Look how flexible I am, how willing to reconsider. When really, no version of the story has settled long enough to close.

My last piece was about time, acceptance, and decision. Time does its slow widening. Acceptance stops arguing with what happened. Decision is the one I keep deferring, because deciding feels like betraying whichever version of me last changed his mind. But the fifty versions don't all get a vote. They get a hearing. Then I get a choice.

Co-Star's Do list had documentaries and scratch paper. The Don't list warned against knee-jerk reactions and small talk. Nothing about texting, which feels like an oversight. I'll take it as restraint.

Fifty-one, if I'm honest. But who's counting.
