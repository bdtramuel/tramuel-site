An old friend called me out of the blue today. We've known each other since elementary school. She was feeling down. Her word for it was purpose, or the lack of one.

On paper her life is full. Love, family, people who would show up for her without being asked. But she's been circling a question a lot of us know. Whether the work she does counts as the meaningful kind, as if there's some other version of her days that would finally qualify. The questions get heavier at this stage of life.

I know because I wrote about that weight in December... [*On Forming Purpose*](https://www.tramuel.com/iam/on-forming-purpose)

I recognized the loop immediately, because it was mine. For years I kept trying to give purpose an address. A job title. A volunteer schedule. Something I could point to and say, there it is, that's the reason. I worked at the Y believing it was filling that space. I searched for a farm to give my time to. All of it was searching. None of it was the thing.

So I told her what I'd finally told someone close to me only a day before her call. That purpose isn't found. It's formed. That it was never hidden in some room I hadn't stumbled into yet. It was already present in the ordinary things. Caring. Showing up. Paying attention. Staying curious. I had just never trusted those things enough to call them purpose, because they didn't collapse into a tidy identity.

Her life isn't unfinished. Neither was mine. We were both just measuring meaning by outcomes, and when the payoff is always later, the present feels thin.

I don't know if it helped. I think it did. But what she said next is the part I keep returning to.

She reached back to 1988. Senior skip day. A girl and I had been talking, spending time together the way friends do. Her boyfriend heard something from someone close to him and decided it meant more than it did. Classic story. Jealousy, possession, somebody's pride doing the thinking. He confronted me in front of everyone, screaming, wanting a fight, crowd forming the way crowds do.

I stood there with my arms crossed and let him yell. I figured he'd been drinking, and I've never needed to prove anything with my hands, though I could have. I didn't escalate. I didn't talk back reckless. I just stayed in my body and waited for it to pass.

I'd mostly filed that day away. She never did. Thirty-eight years later she named it. Not quiet, not passive. A stillness. A calm she believes I've always had, something soft and gentle in how I move through things. She'd been carrying that observation since we were seventeen, and she handed it back to me like it was obvious.

Here is what that did to me. I spent decades searching for the thing, and the whole time it was visible to other people. A friend saw it in a teenager in 1988. It took me until now to see it in myself. The purpose was never missing. I was just the last one to be introduced.

Maybe that's what friends who've known you that long are for. Not to tell you who to become. To remind you who you've been.

I Remain
