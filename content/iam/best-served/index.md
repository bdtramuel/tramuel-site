Karen asked me where my energy would be best served. I didn’t have an answer, and I’ve been carrying the question around since.

We’d spent most of the session on time and acceptance, the way they work after something ends, romantic or platonic. Then she brought up a third thing: decision. That’s the one I keep coming back to.

Time, I thought, I understood. What it gives you is distance, the slow widening of space between who you were with someone and who you are without them. That counts for something. It isn’t the same as being finished.

Acceptance is slower. It gets mistaken for a shrug, but it’s closer to the moment you stop arguing with what happened.

After the session, all of it kept showing up in conversations in my feed, even on Co-Star. I could have called it coincidence. I sat with it instead.

Decision is where I got stuck. Ambiguity is good at passing for patience. I can tell myself I’m waiting on time to settle something, or for acceptance to arrive on its own schedule, when what’s missing is a choice I haven’t made.

Some of the endings this year I chose, and what’s left of those is mine to live with. Others I’ve left open. There’s grief in ending something. There’s also grief in almost ending something, and that kind stretches out over months. It spends the same energy every day and never buys anything back. A door held open is a door I can’t walk away from.

Sitting with a question is sometimes the right thing. Not everything needs an answer by Friday. But sitting with something and hiding behind it can look the same from the outside, and lately I’ve had to ask which one I’m doing.

Karen asked why I’m so comfortable sitting in the gap. I told her I sometimes confuse happiness with isolation.

The only thing I said I wanted in that session was love. To love and to be loved. I hadn’t said it out loud before, not even to myself.
